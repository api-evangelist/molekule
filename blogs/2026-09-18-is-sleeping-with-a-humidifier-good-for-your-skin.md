Key Takeaways Dry indoor air increases how much water your skin loses overnight through a process called transepidermal water loss (TEWL). Keeping bedroom humidity between 40 and 60% supports your skin barrier and reduces overnight moisture loss. Raising humidity from very low levels to around 45% can significantly reduce TEWL, helping your skin retain moisture while you sleep.
